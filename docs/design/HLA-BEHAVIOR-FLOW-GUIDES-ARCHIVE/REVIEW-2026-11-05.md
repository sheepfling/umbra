## 2025 configured-process ordinary receive-order interaction path — 2026-11-05

**Question:** How do an ordinary configured-process receive-order interaction's
transport admission, callback model, and pending-time gate compose, and where
does `HLAsetSwitches` leave that route?

**Evidence:** For ordinary interaction sends, the process service plans
recipient events, then either pushes an unsolicited frame or queues an event
for a receive poll. The client buffers pushed events during a correlated
request, releases that request after its matching response, and later submits
buffered events to the bridge; a poll-returned interaction follows a separate
direct bridge route. The bridge applies the receive-order predicate when the
callback task runs. A pending process time advance opens the receive-order
gate before the grant; it is not evidence that logical time advanced. The
callback model then determines inline `HLA_IMMEDIATE` execution or
application-driven `HLA_EVOKED` dispatch.

The two focused process asynchronous-delivery cases verify the pending-advance
and async-enable behavior under both callback models. They do not cover a
pushed ordinary interaction arriving during an active Evoke wait; that combined
interleaving remains source-derived. `HLAsetSwitches` is decoded and consumed
by the service before ordinary recipient planning, on a separate MOM control
path.

Management guides already contain complementary diagrams for sender/recipient
